Erewhon Los Angeles — visitor patterns, marketing and retail expansion

Four maps and three charts, in the order a decision gets made: who visits the 13 stores today, what kind of places those visitors come from, where a marketing campaign should reach, and what two candidate store locations would be worth.

Data cartobq.docs · BigQuery
Grid H3 resolution 7, ~5.16 km² per cell · 2,937 cells in the area of interest
Chain 13 stores · 4,566,644 visits · 1,925 distinct origin cells

1 · Who visits the stores today

The starting point is the observed data: how many visits each H3 cell sends, how many each store receives, and how tightly each store's catchment is packed.

The 60% trade area on the map is built by taking each store's origin cells in descending order of visits and keeping the smallest set that accounts for 60% of its visitors. It answers "where does the bulk of this store's business actually come from", which a fixed-radius ring cannot.

The trade areas are wildly different sizes. Glendale reaches 60% of its visitors with 8 cells, a single compact blob averaging 3.0 km from the store. 12833 Ventura Blvd needs 44 cells averaging 14.0 km, the furthest-reaching set in the chain, scattered across 15 separate patches of the Valley. Beverly Hills takes the most cells, 55.

Calabasas is the one to watch on volume. Its 60% set is 49 cells averaging 11.7 km, and it covers 516,269 visits — more than any other store's, and more than twice Ventura Blvd's 397,148. It is a large catchment that is also a busy one, which is the combination worth protecting.

7660 Beverly Blvd is the opposite case, and the one that shows why the two averages are both in the table. Its cells average 10.7 km unweighted but only 5.3 km once weighted by visits — a ratio of 2.0×, the widest gap in the chain. Its volume sits close to the store while its footprint straggles a long way out. Same grid, same city, same proposition.

Two different averages are worth keeping apart. Mean km here is the plain average across the cells in the 60% set, which is what the map shows you. The visit-weighted average — where a cell sending 40,000 visitors counts 40,000 times — is always lower, because the busiest cells sit closest: Ventura Blvd is 14.0 km unweighted against 8.7 km weighted. Max km is in the table for completeness but should not be read as size; it is a single cell, and for 7660 Beverly Blvd that cell sits 132.5 km out past Temecula carrying 484 visits, 0.24% of the store's total, while the next-furthest cell in its set is at 20.3 km.

2 · What kind of places the visitors come from

Visit counts say how much. PlacePulse embeddings say what kind. Each H3 cell carries a 256-dimension vector built from sociodemographics, business mix and income, and the workflow reads that vector four ways.

The five layers on map 24 embedding readings + observed visits
  • Visualization — the 256 dimensions compressed to three principal components, painted as red, green and blue. Similar colour means similar vector.
  • Mean deviation — how far a cell sits from the average cell in the area of interest. The loud end is an anomaly.
  • Neighbourhood deviation — how far a cell sits from its own ring of neighbours. The loud end is a seam, where one kind of place stops and another starts.
  • Clustering — the origin cells grouped into five kinds of place by their embeddings alone. The clustering never sees a coordinate or a visit count, so the geographic coherence of the result is emergent.
  • Total visitors per cell — the observed counts from section 1, on the same grid, so the embedding readings can be checked against actual demand.

Open on its own: clausa.app.carto.com/map/1fe69cad-cb7c-45a9-b93e-fcc6068783fa

The clusters come out geographically clean even though the clustering never saw a coordinate. Feed it nothing but 256-dimension embeddings and it separates Los Angeles into the Westside, the Valley, the Calabasas–Agoura corridor, Downtown and east, and a far outer ring. That the groups are contiguous on the map is a result, not an input.

Demand is concentrated in one of them, and it is not the biggest. The Westside core — Culver City through Beverlywood, the smallest cluster on the ground — carries well over half the chain's visits. The Agoura Hills and Westlake corridor covers nearly twice as much ground and sends a twentieth as many visitors per cell. Area on this map tells you almost nothing about where the customers are.

Distance sorts by cluster, not by geography alone. The Westside cells send visitors a short way and the outer ring sends them a long way, with Downtown and the Valley in between — so the kind of place someone lives in predicts how far they will travel better than how far out they happen to sit.

The loudest cells in the embedding are infrastructure, not customers. Turn on mean deviation and the extremes are the LAX airfield, the El Segundo refinery strip and the stadium district around Hollywood Park — places with almost no resident population and an industrial business base. They are genuinely unusual.

2.1 · How far visitors travel

Distance from origin cell to the store it feeds
Box spans the weighted 25th–75th percentile, line is the weighted median, whiskers the weighted 5th and 95th, diamond the weighted mean. Every quantile is weighted by visitor counts, not by cell counts — a cell sending 40,000 visitors counts 40,000 times.

By store the medians run 2.79 km at Glendale to 11.93 km at Calabasas. By cluster the separation is far cleaner: cluster 4 has a median of 4.67 km and cluster 3 a median of 24.96 km, a 5.3× gap that no pair of stores comes close to. The kind of place a visitor comes from predicts how far they travel better than which store they visit does.

What follows from that is a split in what the stores are for. Glendale and West Hollywood are neighbourhood stores: their customers come from nearby and nowhere else. Calabasas and Ventura Blvd are destination stores, pulling people across the Valley and holding a long tail of much longer journeys. The rest sit between the two. Treating the estate as one format, with one catchment assumption behind every site decision, does not match how these stores actually behave — and because origin cluster separates distance more sharply than store identity does, the cluster a place belongs to is the better unit for planning than the store nearest to it.

2.2 · Which kinds of place each store draws from

Each store's visits, split by the origin cluster they came from
Full-width bars, weighted by visits. A bar dominated by one colour means the store draws from a single kind of place.

Twelve of the thirteen stores are dominated by cluster 4, between 43.7% at Pasadena and 84.7% at West Hollywood. Calabasas is the exception: cluster 4 gives it only 26.6%, while cluster 1 gives it 31.9% and cluster 2 a further 17.1%. It is the one store in the chain drawing primarily on a kind of place no other store depends on.

So the chain is built on a single kind of origin place. That is a strength where cluster 4 is dense and a concentration risk everywhere else: twelve of the thirteen stores are competing for the same population, and anything that changes the Westside — housing costs, a competitor, a shift in who lives there — moves most of the estate at once. Calabasas is the only evidence in the data that an Erewhon can work off a different kind of origin place, which makes it the most informative store in the chain for anyone considering where to open next.

3 · Where to grow

Both growth questions run off the same object: a visitor-weighted embedding profile per store. Take every origin cell that feeds a store, weight its embedding by the visits it sends, average them, and normalise to unit length. The result is one 256-dimension vector describing the kind of place that store's customers come from.

3.1 · How alike the store profiles are

Pairwise cosine between the 13 store profiles
All 13 stores, in one order. The diagonal is marked as self, not coloured.
The profiles are nearly identical, and that is the finding. Across the 78 store pairs the cosine runs from 0.9076 to 0.9977, with a median of 0.9813, and 55 of the 78 sit above 0.97. Erewhon is not running thirteen propositions; it is running one, in thirteen places. The two extremes are worth naming: Calabasas ↔ West Hollywood at 0.9076 is the most distinct pair in the chain, and Beverly Hills ↔ Santa Monica at 0.9977 is the least. Calabasas appears in both of the two lowest pairs, which is consistent with what section 2.2 showed about its origin mix.

3.2 · Marketing expansion — where to advertise

Map 3 scores every cell in the area of interest on two things and combines them. Similarity is the average cosine between the cell's embedding and the 13 store profiles. Current visits counts visitors going to those stores. The composite score standard-scales both, weights them equally and reverses visits, so a high score marks a place that looks like an Erewhon customer base and does not shop there yet.

The map has three readings, not two. Red is opportunity: places that look like Erewhon customers and send few. Pale near the stores is the opposite of opportunity — it is territory already won, and the bottom-bucket cells within 15 km of a branch average 23,113 visits, the highest counts in the chain. Pale far from the stores is neither: 462 of the 585 bottom-bucket cells send nobody at all, because almost nobody lives there. Only the first is a media plan; the second is a retention question and the third is nothing.

Every one of the target (red) cells already sends visitors: the model is pointing at demand that exists rather than demand it hopes for. Of the cells an existing store could actually serve, four pockets stand out:

  • San Gabriel Valley, east of Pasadena — South Pasadena and San Marino through Alhambra, Arcadia, Monrovia and Duarte. By far the largest pocket, starting 2.7 km from the Pasadena store, and holding the strongest cell on the whole map: cosine 0.975 with 3,839 visits already.
  • North San Fernando Valley — Van Nuys, Panorama City, Northridge and Porter Ranch, 7 to 16 km from 12833 Ventura Blvd. The Van Nuys cell is the second strongest anywhere at 0.973 and 3,818 visits.
  • South Bay — Torrance, Gardena and the Palos Verdes edge, 10 to 16 km from Manhattan Beach, consistently around 0.93–0.96 with one to two thousand visits per cell.
  • La Cañada Flintridge, 4.5 km north of Glendale — a single cell, but a strong one at 0.959 and 2,424 visits, and the closest thing to an easy win on the map.
Set the distance slider before reading the map as a media plan. The composite ranks on similarity and current visits only — distance to a store is not one of its inputs, so the unfiltered top bucket averages 44.2 km from the nearest branch, with 21 of its 589 cells inside 10 km and 58 inside 15 km. The KM to nearest store widget applies that constraint directly, and the four pockets above are what survives it. Left wide open, the same map reads as an argument for opening somewhere new rather than advertising — which is what section 3.3 takes up.

3.3 · Retail expansion — what a new store would be worth

The same machinery answers a different question. Rather than scoring cells for a campaign, a model trained on the existing 13 stores predicts, for each candidate location, how many visitors every cell in the area would send it. Two sites are evaluated: Costa Mesa and Thousand Oaks.

This map opens filtered to Thousand Oaks. Use the Per new store widget to switch between the two candidates, or clear it to see both.

The two sites come out almost level on total predicted demand — 153,171 visits for Costa Mesa against 151,234 for Thousand Oaks, a gap of 1.3%. That is well inside what a model like this can resolve, so the totals alone do not pick a winner. The shape differs more than the total: Thousand Oaks has more cells clearing 100 predicted visits (187 against 179) and a higher median cell (13 against 10), meaning its demand is spread more evenly, while Costa Mesa's is more concentrated.

Both candidates would land in the bottom third of the existing chain by volume — 12833 Ventura Blvd takes 658,054 visits and Calabasas 854,954, while the smallest current store, 9300 Culver Blvd, takes 101,348. On this model a new store at either site would rank just above the chain's smallest.

What the four maps say together

One customer base, thirteen doors. The store profiles sit between 0.908 and 0.998 cosine of each other, median 0.981. Erewhon is not running thirteen propositions; it is running one, in thirteen places.

The kind of place predicts travel better than the store does. Origin clusters separate on median journey by 5.3×, store identity by far less. Cluster 4 — the dense Westside band — carries 2.61M of the chain's 4.57M visits at a median of 4.67 km.

The marketing score finds the right places; distance is the filter you apply to them. High similarity plus low current visits is the correct definition of an opportunity, and every top-bucket cell already sends visitors. Distance is not one of the score's inputs, so the unfiltered ranking spreads to the edge of the study area — but the map carries a km-to-nearest-store slider, so narrowing it to what an existing store can serve is a single adjustment rather than a rebuild.

Which is what makes the retail question the interesting one. If the best-scoring demand is far from the estate, the answer may be a new door rather than a campaign — and Costa Mesa and Thousand Oaks are within 1.3% of each other on predicted volume, so the choice between them should turn on cost, cannibalisation and site quality rather than on this model.

Method notes

  • Similarity rescale. The Similarity Search component reports (1 + cosine) / 2. All cosine figures here are converted back with 2 × similarity_score − 1.
  • Weighting. Travel-distance quantiles and the store profiles are visitor-weighted. The origin clustering is not — see the note in section 2.
  • Distances are geodesic, not drive-time, and should be read as a lower bound on travel effort.
  • Trade areas (erewhon_store_coverage_60) are the smallest set of origin cells covering 60% of a store's visits, not a fixed radius.
  • Predictions (results_predict) come from a model fitted on the existing 13 stores, so they assume a new store behaves like the current estate. Both candidates share an identical maximum cell value of 4,764 predicted visits, which suggests a cap or a shared dominant cell in the model — worth checking before the numbers are used for a business case.
